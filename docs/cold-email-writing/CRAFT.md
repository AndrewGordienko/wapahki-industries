# Cold-email craft — distilled from the 14 reference transcripts

The full distillation (7 reader agents across the transcripts → one synthesis). A condensed,
bans-obeying version of this is wired into `playbooks/_shared.md` so the writer for all three
campaigns uses it. This file is the fuller reference.

## Core principles
- **Get a reply, not a sale.** You can't book anything if no one responds. Optimize every line to earn a yes/no/objection — silence is the only unworkable outcome.
- **Write about them, not you.** Educate them on the *problem you solve*, not the solution you sell. "I help you," never "we help people." Speak their lingo, not marketing puff.
- **The offer carries the email.** Fix the offer before the words: quantified outcome + time frame + guarantee/risk reversal + "new money." Make saying no feel irrational; put all risk on you.
- **Personal beats personalized.** Manual, human-sounding emails out-reply automation ~10x. Pass the text-message test: would a friend think you typed this just to them?
- **~80% of the win is outside the writing** — tight list, one segment, one trigger, a template per persona. And 70% of booked meetings come from follow-ups, not the first send.

## The opener (first line)
Lead with a specific, true observation — never "hope this finds you well." Buy 30 seconds of attention.
- **Situation/truth line (personal, not personalized):** "Steve, end of the month and lots of commissions to pay." / "Guessing your landscaping got torn up by the storm."
- **Label + presuppose (Chris Voss):** "It looks like you're hiring two systems analysts and still on [legacy system]." If wrong, they'll correct you.
- **Trigger + relevance:** "Saw you were hiring SDRs on LinkedIn — we cut hiring costs and book qualified meetings pay-per-performance."
- **Credibility opener (senior buyers):** "I spoke with [name] and understand [X] is a big focus for you this year."

Director/VP/C-suite care about the *company*, not that you know their college. ICs respond to personal detail.

## Structure of a strong email
Keep it 4–5 sentences / ~75 words, one phone screen, one idea:
1. **Observation** — the true situation/trigger.
2. **Think question** — reframe their current painful process (mine the exact words from a case study).
3. **Proof** — a peer's before→after with real numbers: "Gong runs commissions in 5 minutes instead of 5 hours, no rebuilding sheets, no manual entry."
4. **Cost of doing nothing**, quantified when possible.
5. **Soft CTA.**

Match the emotion to the buyer: FOMO/ego for founders and sales/marketing leaders; ROI/stats for technical buyers.

## Specificity and relevance (find, don't write)
- **Don't write copy — find it.** Lift the buyer's own words from case studies, testimonials, reviews (grab visceral words like "hate," "nightmare"). Paste them into your framework so they think "you get me."
- **Build a messaging matrix from the buyer's lens:** priorities, current solutions, problems, aspirations — never your features.
- **Pick ONE signal and go deep.** Three shallow signals in one email loses to one specific problem told well.
- Personalization must ladder back to the problem, or it's box-ticking ("I noticed you have money, buy my stuff").

## The ask / CTA
- **One ask, its own line, before the sign-off.** No "is this worth a chat?", "let me know your thoughts," "if you're interested."
- **Make an offer instead of asking for time** (asking for time drops replies ~28%): "I'll put you in touch with Dave, a decade working with VPs of Manufacturing like you; you'll learn what's working with the labor market."
- **Or ask for a yes/no, not a meeting:** "I'm seeing this could be of interest, am I right?" / "Worth a conversation?" / "Worth a look? No rush."
- **No links in cold emails.** On LinkedIn, warm up first and stay conversational — don't pitch on connect.

## Subject lines
- **2–4 words, lowercase, no product/hype** — look like an internal email. One job: earn the open.
- Good: "quick question" · "estimates" · "2026?" · "Nick's blind spot" · "your streaming bill" · "been with you from the start."
- Rule of three (expected + expected + unexpected): "Abdul + podcast + mother-in-law."
- Bad: "Interested in AI-driven performance optimization", "10x your revenue", or no subject at all.

## Hard DON'Ts
- No pitching features — only the ~5% already shopping bite.
- No asking for a meeting or a price in the first touch. No links.
- No "hope this finds you well," "just bumping this," "did you get my last email," or opt-out footers.
- No cutesy fake personalization ("saw we're both dog dads"), no walls of text, no illustrious signatures, no branding yourself as AI.
- Don't overclaim — small credible numbers beat big ones.
- Don't send from your main domain; don't chase past 3–4 touches; don't automate before you've cracked it manually.

## Steal-these phrasings
- **POV skeleton:** "Saw [observation]. That usually means [insight]. Issue is [problem]. Have you looked into [category] before?"
- **Braun 4-part:** "[Situation]. Curious, how are you dealing with [painful process, in their words]? [Peer] uses [product] to [outcome] in [X instead of Y], no more [pains]. Worth a conversation?"
- **Offer line:** "We book 15–25 qualified demos/month for [segment] in 90 days, or you don't pay."
- **Best follow-up line, period:** "Please give me your thoughts on this." (Follow-ups stay short and point back to email #1, never re-pitch.)
- **Metaphor beats adjective:** "Calculating commissions in spreadsheets feels like sipping peanut butter through a straw."
- **Soft closes:** "Worth a look?" · "Worth an exchange?" · "Is this on your agenda?" · "Am I right?"
