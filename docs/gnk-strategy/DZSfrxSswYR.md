## Spoken thesis

- A large share of Western economic ownership sits with the baby-boom
  generation.
- Many of those owners are changing how they want to live and work: selling a
  business, delegating daily operations, moving into an advisory role, or
  freeing time for other priorities.
- That transition creates three broad plays: acquire businesses changing hands,
  build a challenger to an old operating model, or sell useful services to the
  people and businesses going through the transition.
- The strategic lesson is to follow a large transition in ownership, work and
  spending rather than chase only the newest technology trend.

## What GNK takes from it

GNK will pursue the third play: help established owner-operated businesses
become less dependent on the owner for routine operating work. AI is an
implementation tool. The buyer-facing outcome is more owner time, documented
operating knowledge, faster handoffs and a business that can keep running as
responsibility moves to managers or a successor.

This is a business-lifecycle thesis, not an age-targeting tactic:

- Never infer a person's age, retirement plan, health, wealth or intention to
  sell.
- Never use `baby boomer`, `older owner`, `retirement` or similar language in
  outreach unless the company itself has made a directly relevant transition
  public—and even then, discuss the business event, not the person's age.
- Scout from public transition and operating signals: a stated succession or
  ownership change, a new president/GM/COO, an owner seeking operational
  leadership, a modernization project, repeated hiring around a bottleneck, an
  acquisition integration, or a documented knowledge-continuity problem.
- Do not diagnose “owner dependence” from the founder's title alone. Name one
  source-backed workflow hypothesis and invite correction.

## Canadian corroboration

- ISED reports that more than 17% of Canadian SME owners plan to exit within
  five years, based on the 2023 Survey on Financing and Growth of SMEs:
  https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/ised/en/programs-and-initiatives/business-succession-planning
- BDC's 2026 study says transitions are expected in nearly one in five Canadian
  businesses within five years:
  https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/55529-m-and-a-advantage-canada-entrepreneurs-study.pdf
- BDC recommends starting succession planning well before an exit and identifies
  training a successor and improving business operations as part of the work:
  https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/change-ownership/plan-succession/3-common-exit-strategies

The reel's exact economy-ownership percentage is not used as a GNK targeting
fact. The Canadian evidence is strong enough to support the lane without it.
