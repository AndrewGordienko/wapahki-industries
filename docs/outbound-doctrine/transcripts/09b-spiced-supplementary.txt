So, let's break down the six components of spiced. The S stands for situation. Discovery starts here. No pitch, no assumptions, just context. You're uncovering how the business runs, who they serve, what tools they use, what pressures they're under. You can ask, "How does your team currently manage X processes, what tools are part of your stack right now, any recent changes in the business or strategy?" Listen for clues, but also listen for emotion, stress, frustration, excitement. Those are your bridge to the next phase. P is for pain. This is where things get real. What's broken? What's frustrating? Maybe their processes are too manual. Maybe their current solution is clunky. Maybe the team's burnt out. Don't rush this. Instead, go deeper. What's the biggest challenge with that setup? What happens when that goes wrong? In an ideal world, how would this work instead? Your job isn't just to find the pain. It's to understand it better than they do. I refers to impact. Pain gets attention. Impact gets buyin. Once you understand the pain, now ask what does that cost them in time, morale, money, revenue, growth. This is how you link a product to their outcomes. Ask how do these challenges affect the team's productivity or customer satisfaction? What happens to revenue when those delays stack up? No impact equals no urgency. Find the business case behind the frustration. C means critical event. This is where you focus on the timeline, not your deadline, their real world trigger. What's forcing this deal to happen now? A leadership mandate, a quarterly review, compliance. Ask when do you need this to be resolved by? What happens if it's not fixed by then? Test the date. If there's no consequence, it's not critical. So, keep digging. And finally, D stands for decision. The deal lives or dies here. You need clarity on who, how, and when the decision gets made. Who else is involved in this decision? What criteria are most important when evaluating solutions? Have you purchased something like this before? What did that process look like? This is where you build your map of influence, navigate blockers, and prep your champion for the internal pitch. So, that's the spice method broken down. But how do you master it?
